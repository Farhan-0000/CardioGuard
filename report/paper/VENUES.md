## 5. Preprints

- **A preprint is not proof of peer-reviewed submission.** arXiv and medRxiv only screen submissions; they do not peer-review them. Use a preprint for visibility and a timestamp, not for Review-4 item 9.
- **arXiv (recommended):** category cs.CL, cross-listed to cs.AI and cs.IR.
  - First-time submitters need **endorsement**. Some accounts are endorsed automatically through an institutional email address plus claimed papers. Otherwise ask a guide or co-author who is an arXiv endorser in cs.
  - Since late 2025, arXiv cs requires prior peer review for **review and position papers only**. Original research such as MedBridge is unaffected.
- **medRxiv (optional, borderline fit):**
  - It accepts human-health research, including "methodological research/investigations", and says it "welcomes computational papers" if they are full research papers with methods and results.
  - MedBridge has no human-participant data and is essentially a system plus benchmark evaluation. It may still be in scope as health-informatics methods research, but screeners could decline it or redirect it.
  - It requires ethics, funding, competing-interest and data-availability statements, and declines work that could endanger patients. A patient-facing guidance tool must state clearly that it is not for diagnosis.
  - Screening usually takes 2–4 days.
  - Post to **one** server only; arXiv is the safer choice.
- **Timing:**
  - Post the preprint *after* submitting to the conference.
  - For double-blind venues (BIBM posters are double-blind; AIHC said double-blind), check the anonymity rules before posting.
  - IEEE allows authors to post the submitted version. After acceptance, add the IEEE copyright notice and the DOI.

## 6. What counts as proof of submission (Review-4 item 9)

**Strong proof:**
1. The **automatic confirmation email** from the submission system, showing the Paper ID, title, author list, venue or track, and a timestamp. Examples:
   - Microsoft CMT: "Submission … has been created"
   - EasyChair: "EasyChair submission N"
   - Journal systems: JMIR, ScholarOne, Editorial Manager or SNAPP manuscript number
   - BIBM CyberChair: confirmation email with paper ID

   Print it to PDF with the full headers.
2. A **screenshot of the Author Console**:
   - CMT: "Author Console", showing Paper ID, title, status ("Awaiting Decision" or similar), with the browser URL bar visible.
   - EasyChair: "My Submissions".

   Also export or print the submission summary page.
3. The **submitted PDF** exactly as uploaded, with its file timestamp.
4. Later on, these are even stronger: the **acceptance letter**, the **registration or payment receipt** (to the official account named on the venue site), and the **IEEE copyright form or PDF eXpress confirmation**.

**Weak or invalid proof:**
- An arXiv or medRxiv posting on its own.
- A Google-Form acknowledgement with no paper ID (for example, the ICAIET poster form).
- A payment receipt to a publisher that "guarantees" publication.
- An email you wrote yourself saying "paper submitted".
- A submission to a venue with no visible peer-review process.

**Good practice:**
- List all team members and the guide as authors *before* the deadline. AIHC, for example, does not allow authors to be added after the deadline, and many IEEE conferences have the same rule.
- Use institutional email addresses.
- Keep proof for every round.
- **Never** submit the same manuscript to two venues at once to "collect" proofs. This breaks IEEE, Springer and ACL dual-submission policies and can lead to bans. To change venue, withdraw formally first.

## 7. Predatory-venue checklist

Before you submit anywhere not in the top 5, run through this list. Two or more "yes" answers is a red flag.

1. **Indexing claims you cannot verify.**
   - "Scopus indexed" for a conference that is not actually in an IEEE, Springer, ACM or ACL series.
   - "Submitted to IEEE Xplore" with **no IEEE Conference Record number** and no IEEE section or society sponsor.
   - Check the record number in the IEEE conference search, and check that past proceedings exist in IEEE Xplore or SpringerLink.
2. **Guaranteed or very fast acceptance**: for example "acceptance within 48 hours", "100% publication", or acceptance before any review.
3. **No real peer-review process:** no named Technical Program Committee, inconsistent review statements, or no reviewer comments returned.
4. **Payment before acceptance**, or fees much higher than similar Indian IEEE conferences (₹5,000–9,000 is typical for students).
5. **Organiser is a private event company** rather than a university or a society chapter or section. Many-topic "multi-conference" umbrellas.
6. **Copied or stale websites**: other conferences' names in page titles, previous years' dates, broken links, or only Gmail or WhatsApp contacts.
7. **Proceedings published only by the organiser's own press**, or "three publications for one registration" bundling.
8. **Resort or tourist venues** marketed more than the science; "best paper guaranteed" style awards.
9. **Unsolicited email invitations** that flatter the authors or offer editorial-board seats.
10. **Journal checks:**
    - Is it in DOAJ, and in Scopus (Scopus Sources list) or Web of Science Master Journal List?
    - Is it a COPE member?
    - Is the APC transparent?
    - Watch out for hijacked journal websites: compare the URL against the Scopus and WoS entry.

**How the venues above score:**
- **Very low:** BIBM, BHI, EMBC, ML4H, ACL workshops.
- **Low:** ICITIIT, SCES, ICAIET, INDICON, CONECCT.
- **Low–moderate:** AIHC (sloppy but IEEE-sponsored), ICDSA.
- **Moderate:** AiMH (self-published proceedings, abstract-based acceptance), ICT4SD (commercial organiser, high fee), AIEHT (no IEEE record number yet).
