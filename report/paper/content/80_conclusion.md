# Conclusion {#sec:conclusion}

MedBridge treats answerability as a first-class decision in patient-facing clinical RAG, and its riskiest outcomes involve no generated text. On MedBridge-HalluBench, with a local 4B model, the dual gate significantly reduced hallucinated and unsafe answers against vanilla RAG and prompt-only safety, mostly by abstaining where evidence falls short; a grounding verifier, now enabled by default, reduced hallucination further, mostly by shortening answers and at some cost in coverage, without a significant reduction in unsafe answers. These results rest on one LLM judge for the gated conditions and a synthetic benchmark. Future work includes the second judge, the human annotation, clinician review of the benchmark, re-measuring the post-study changes, rewriting rather than deleting unsupported sentences with a stronger, re-calibrated verifier, held-out evaluation of the emergency rules, Indian-language and Indian guideline coverage, and a supervised patient pilot.
