**What the gates do.** The dual gate makes the system safer by deciding when not to answer, not by making its answers more faithful: it declines, generalises or asks more often, but the clinical content it lets through is hallucinated as often as without gates ([@fig:composition]). This is selective abstention [@geifman2017selective] at a cost of about a quarter of the answerable turns. Asking for the same behaviour in prose (C2) achieves much less (appropriate abstention {{study.per_condition.C2.appropriate_abstention|pct}} against {{study.per_condition.C5.appropriate_abstention|pct}} for C5). Most of the benefit comes from the Evidence Gate; Gate 1, although designed to let unanswerable questions proceed, asked for clarification on 11 of the 66 unanswerable C5 turns and adds only a small, fragile reduction: the gates differ in kind (asking versus declining) more than in effect.

**Failure modes.** In C5, Gate 1 proceeded on 12 turns that needed a clarifying question, mostly by reading personal questions ("what blood pressure should I be aiming for?") as general ones (three, where the patient declines to give details, are debatable), and Gate 2 chose ANSWER on 4 missing-context turns. Of the 10 remaining unsafe answers, 9 came through GENERALIZE, five of them correctly routed false-premise questions whose general reply still carried an unsupported claim. The dominant problem is generation itself, which citation validation cannot catch and V2 targets.

**Post-study changes.** The dual gate stays the core of MedBridge, and V1 stays off. Because V2 significantly reduced hallucination, Gate 3 is now enabled by default (a decision taken after the study, outside the pre-specified rule, that accepts V2's lower coverage) with its development-calibrated thresholds (θ = {{calibration.v2.thresholds.GROUNDING_ENTAILMENT_THRESHOLD|f1}}, τ = {{calibration.v2.thresholds.GROUNDING_MAX_UNSUPPORTED_FRACTION|f1}}; commit 7a3bc67), and since commit 16e594b it withdraws a draft whose every claim is unsupported instead of delivering it unchanged (in the V2 test runs, 16 turns, 13 of them judged hallucinated; not re-measured). Deleting sentences has a usability cost, the coverage side of the trade-off measured in [@sec:results] (fewer claims, lower coverage): after the study, "My blood pressure was 145/92 this morning. Is that high?" lost five of its six claims at θ = 0.2, and as the remaining sentence had no citation, the citation rule made it a one-sentence GENERALIZE; stripped answers can also start mid-thought (remedies: [@sec:conclusion]). A live brand-versus-generic test (RABBITS, {{rabbits.pairs}} pairs, {{rabbits.items}} items, Gate 3 off in both runs) exposed fragile brand names. A fix (commit 02d9774, re-run at fb38cf6) gives the extractor, both gates and the generator lexicon notes mapping brands to generic ingredients, adds a deterministic current-medication backstop and filters drug names in the search query; action consistency rose from {{rabbits_before.action_consistency_pct|perc}} to {{rabbits.action_consistency_pct|perc}} (Indian brands {{rabbits_before.by_origin.indian.action_consistency_pct|perc}} to {{rabbits.by_origin.indian.action_consistency_pct|perc}}) and stored-generic agreement from {{rabbits_before.extraction_consistency_pct|perc}} to {{rabbits.extraction_consistency_pct|perc}}. A live 19-payload prompt-injection suite passed every check before and after the fix (after it with Gate 3 on).
