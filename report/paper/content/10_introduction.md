# Introduction {#sec:intro}

Hypertension is a major and largely preventable driver of cardiovascular disease, yet many affected adults worldwide remain undiagnosed, untreated or uncontrolled [@who2023globalreport; @ncdrisc2021hypertension]; in India, a national survey found it in roughly one adult in three [@anjana2023indiab]. Control depends on what patients understand between visits about their readings, medicines and lifestyle, and many now ask conversational AI. Large language models (LLMs) hold considerable clinical knowledge [@singhal2023llmclinical] but also produce confident statements that no source supports, usually called hallucinations [@ji2023survey].

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) conditions the model on passages from a trusted corpus [@lewis2020rag] and has improved medical question answering [@xiong2024medrag]. A standard RAG pipeline, however, is built to answer whatever the quality of the match. Three routine situations in patient conversations defeat this design. A question can be answerable in principle while the safe answer depends on facts the patient has not given, such as a dose change for an unnamed medicine. A question can fall outside the evidence: a drug that does not exist, a false premise, a dose no guideline states, a topic outside cardiology. And a message can describe an emergency, where a long reply only delays care. Asking the generator to "say when it does not know" leaves the decision to the component most prone to over-confident output, although knowing when not to answer is a capability in its own right [@wen2025abstention].

MedBridge instead makes answerability an explicit decision taken by separate components at fixed points around retrieval, each logged with its rationale. Deterministic rules handle emergencies before any model call. A *Context Gate* (Gate 1) judges before retrieval whether the patient context suffices or a clarifying question is needed. An *Evidence Gate* (Gate 2) judges after retrieval whether the passages support a specific answer, only general information, or nothing, and whether to escalate. Refusals and escalations never pass through the generator, and all models run locally, so no patient text leaves the device. Our contributions are:

1. the *Dual Answerability Gate*, a five-action routing architecture (ANSWER, SOFT-ASK, GENERALIZE, ABSTAIN, ESCALATE) that separates missing *context* from missing *evidence*, with a deterministic emergency override and template bypass;
2. two strictly conservative extensions: a calibrated Evidence Gate and a post-generation grounding verifier ("Gate 3");
3. MedBridge-HalluBench, a synthetic benchmark of casual, multi-turn patient questions labelled with the expected safe behaviour, and a calibration-gated evaluation protocol;
4. an open, fully local implementation sized for a 6 GB laptop GPU.
