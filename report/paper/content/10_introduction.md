# Introduction {#sec:intro}

Hypertension is a major, largely preventable driver of cardiovascular disease, and many affected adults remain undiagnosed, untreated or uncontrolled [@who2023globalreport]; in India, a national survey found it in roughly one adult in three [@anjana2023indiab]. Between visits, many patients now ask conversational AI about their readings, medicines and lifestyle. Large language models (LLMs) hold considerable clinical knowledge [@singhal2023llmclinical] but also make confident statements that no source supports, usually called hallucinations [@ji2023survey].

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) conditions the model on passages from a trusted corpus [@lewis2020rag] and has improved medical question answering [@xiong2024medrag], but a standard RAG pipeline answers whatever the quality of the match. Three routine situations defeat it: the safe answer depends on facts the patient has not given (a dose change for an unnamed medicine); the question falls outside the evidence (a drug that does not exist, a false premise, a dose no guideline states, a non-cardiac topic); or the message describes an emergency, where a long reply only delays care. Asking the generator to "say when it does not know" leaves the decision to the component most prone to over-confidence, although knowing when not to answer is a distinct capability [@wen2025abstention].

MedBridge instead makes answerability an explicit, logged decision taken at fixed points around retrieval: deterministic rules handle emergencies before any model call, a *Context Gate* (Gate 1) judges before retrieval whether a clarifying question is needed, and an *Evidence Gate* (Gate 2) judges after retrieval whether the passages support a specific answer, only general information or nothing. Refusals and escalations bypass the generator, and all models run locally. Our contributions are:

1. the *Dual Answerability Gate*, a five-action routing architecture (ANSWER, SOFT-ASK, GENERALIZE, ABSTAIN, ESCALATE) that separates missing *context* from missing *evidence*, with a deterministic emergency override;
2. two extensions evaluated as variants, a calibrated Evidence Gate and a conservative post-generation grounding verifier ("Gate 3"), the latter now enabled by default;
3. MedBridge-HalluBench, a synthetic benchmark of casual, multi-turn patient questions labelled with the expected safe behaviour, and a paired ten-condition evaluation;
4. a fully local implementation (FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Qdrant, Ollama) designed for a 6 GB laptop GPU (code release after the patent decision).
