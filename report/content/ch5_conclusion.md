# Conclusion and Future Work

This chapter summarises the work carried out, states the main findings, draws the conclusions of the project and outlines its limitations together with concrete directions for future work.

## Summary of Work Completed

The project set out to build a conversational assistant for hypertension and cardiovascular care that decides, before writing any clinical text, whether it is entitled to answer. The final system, MedBridge, implements this idea as a Dual Answerability Gate inside an eight-step pipeline and completes the scope approved at the earlier reviews. The main pieces of work are the following.

- **Architecture preserved and refactored.** The eight-step workflow, the five actions, both gates at temperature 0.0 with seed 42, the soft-ask loop-breaker at two, the template bypass for ABSTAIN and ESCALATE, XML isolation of patient text, event-sourced state with append-only clinical events and audit logs, and the never-throw orchestrator were all kept. The monolithic orchestrator was rewritten as traced stage methods with a single database flush per turn, per-session serialisation of turns, speculative retrieval that preserves the gate semantics, citation validation with an ANSWER-to-GENERALIZE downgrade, deterministic small-talk handling and correct multi-turn follow-ups after a clarifying question. Every significant change was justified in the Architecture Decision Log.
- **Deterministic emergency handling.** The emergency fast-path was rebuilt as a natural-language rules engine that handles colloquial phrasing, negation, typos, educational and historical mentions and common Hinglish expressions, while remaining deterministic, model-free and within its 10 ms budget.
- **Knowledge base and retrieval.** The corpus grew from 3 PDF guidelines to {{corpus.totals.documents|int}} curated documents with a checksummed manifest and licence notes, and ingestion was rebuilt with tokenizer-measured chunking, table-aware parsing, HTML support and incremental updates. The retrieval design was evaluated on a labelled query set, and the reciprocal rank fusion constant was corrected to the specified value of 60.
- **Local, structured language-model layer.** All inference runs locally through Ollama with grammar-constrained structured outputs, per-role models, truncation handling and a local-only mode that fails closed. Prompts were rewritten for the broader cardiology scope, with explicit rules for false premises, fabricated drugs, unsupported doses, paediatric and pregnancy questions, and with few-shot examples decontaminated against the benchmark. Two variants close to the original design were implemented: a calibrated Evidence Gate (V1) and a post-generation grounding verifier acting as a third gate (V2).
- **Privacy and security by design.** The two limitations listed at Review 1 were removed: personal identifiers are redacted before any model call or database write, and sessions are protected by signed, expiring tokens. Stored content is encrypted with per-session keys, erasure is implemented by crypto-shredding so that the append-only audit trail survives, and security headers, rate limits, request-size limits, log scrubbing and a retention job complete the layer.
- **Interface and features.** The Streamlit prototype was replaced by a chat application served by FastAPI, with streamed pipeline progress, action badges, a citation drawer, a "Why this answer?" panel that exposes the gate decisions, a patient-context panel with a BP chart, a clinician summary export, an administrator dashboard and user-facing privacy controls; the Streamlit client was kept as a fallback.
- **Evaluation infrastructure.** MedBridge-HalluBench, a synthetic benchmark of 247 items with a development and a test split, was created together with an evaluation package that runs nine conditions through the production orchestrator, judges responses with an LLM judge from a different model family and a deterministic NLI judge, calibrates thresholds on the development split only, accounts for degraded turns, memoises identical model calls and computes paired statistics with multiplicity correction. The live MedBridge-AQ, RABBITS, adversarial, smoke and load harnesses were rebuilt to run against the real pipeline instead of a mock.
- **Verification.** A layered automated test suite covers every module and invariant, and an adversarial code review confirmed and fixed 37 defects before the final experiments, several of which would otherwise have biased the hallucination study.

## Conclusion

This project showed that the decision of whether to answer can be made an explicit, auditable part of a retrieval-augmented medical assistant. MedBridge separates the two reasons for holding back, insufficient patient context and insufficient evidence, into two gates at two different points of the pipeline, places a deterministic emergency check in front of both, and routes every message to exactly one of five actions whose provenance is recorded in an immutable audit log and shown to the user. The complete system, including the gates, hybrid retrieval, privacy layer and interface, runs on a single laptop with free, locally hosted models and sends no patient text to any external service.

The component evaluations support several design choices and expose clear limits. Grammar-constrained decoding made the output of small local models reliably parseable, so routing quality, not malformed output, is what distinguishes candidate models. Hybrid retrieval gave the best ranking metrics, clearly ahead of sparse retrieval alone and somewhat ahead of dense retrieval alone (a difference within the confidence intervals), while the cross-encoder reranker did not measurably improve ranking on the labelled set and is retained mainly for its role as an evidence-sufficiency signal. Rule-based redaction proved precise and safe for clinical values but recalled only part of the personal names in a blind test, which sets a clear target for future work.

## Limitations and Future Enhancements

The limitations of the work are discussed in detail at the end of Chapter 4. The most important ones are an incomplete knowledge base in heart failure and coronary disease, a synthetic single-author benchmark without clinician validation, automatic judges that are themselves models, limited recall of personal names by the rule-based redactor, simulated rather than token-level streaming, a single-node design, and the absence of a test on the Windows laptop with the RTX 4050 GPU by the authors of this report. The following enhancements address these limitations directly.

1. **Complete the knowledge base.** Obtain the missing guidelines through a browser download or institutional access (the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA heart-failure guideline, the 2023 ESH guideline, the full 2023 chronic coronary disease guideline and the 2024 ESC guidelines on atrial fibrillation and chronic coronary syndromes), resolve the open licence questions, and add a scheduled update process that re-ingests documents when a new version is published, using the existing checksum and manifest mechanism. Where guidelines disagree, the answer should name each source and its recommendation rather than blending them.
2. **Clinician-validated benchmark.** Have clinicians review the HalluBench gold labels, measure inter-annotator agreement, extend the benchmark with more multi-turn, Hinglish and regional-language items, and complete the human annotation sheet so that both judges are validated against people.
3. **Stronger evaluation.** Use a panel of judges from different families or a larger judge, judge one claim per call to remove batch effects, and repeat the study with the final model on both target machines.
4. **Better-calibrated abstention.** Add a conformal or selective-prediction threshold on gate confidence (a "V3" variant) so that the abstention rate can be set to a target risk level with a statistical guarantee [@angelopoulos2023conformal; @quach2024conformal; @kang2024crag], and evaluate a medical reranker such as MedCPT as the evidence-sufficiency signal.
5. **Grounding verification in production.** If V2 performs well once calibrated, enable the grounding verifier by default and extend it to check numbers and drug names explicitly, since these are the most harmful kinds of unsupported claim.
6. **Real streaming.** Stream the generator's output as it is produced, with sentence-level validation of citations and claims before each sentence is shown, so that the first words appear within seconds while no unvalidated claim reaches the patient.
7. **Stronger privacy.** Add a small local named-entity model or a gazetteer for names without a cue, support UK and other international identifier formats, implement master-key rotation and token revocation, and store the master key in the operating system's key store.
8. **Multilingual use.** Extend natural-language understanding beyond the emergency classifier to Hindi, Tamil and Hinglish questions, for example through local translation before the pipeline and back after it, marked as experimental until evaluated.
9. **Deployment and scale.** Verify installation, performance and accessibility on the Windows 11 target laptop and on further browsers and screen readers; package the application as a container; and support several workers with a shared rate limiter and database-level coordination of the soft-ask counter.
10. **Integration and user studies.** Import readings from home BP monitors, offer an export in a clinical data standard for sharing with a doctor, and run a usability study with patients and clinicians under ethics approval. A prospective clinical evaluation would be required before any real-world use.
