## Live Robustness Benchmarks and Post-Study Checks

These benchmarks ran live through the production pipeline with `qwen3.5:4b` against the evaluation database; each table note names the commit of its run. The AQ benchmark evaluated the code of the hallucination study (82359eb, Gate 3 off). RABBITS and the adversarial suite were run before and after the brand-name fix (`02d9774`). Both pre-fix runs already contained the inference lock (`3f58b59`). The RABBITS re-run kept Gate 3 off, and the adversarial re-run had Gate 3 on (`7a3bc67`) but came before `16e594b` (Section 4.12).

#### MedBridge-AQ Routing Benchmark

Each of the {{aq.metrics.total_vignettes|int}} AQ vignettes seeds a patient snapshot and soft-ask counter and checks the routed action and the cited sources (Table 4.18). The run missed both quality targets of 90 per cent: routing accuracy was {{aq.metrics.overall_routing_accuracy|perc}} and citation precision {{aq.metrics.overall_citation_precision|perc}} (over the {{aq.metrics.citation_evaluated|int}} vignettes that returned citations and list expected sources). It met both safety targets: the fast-path escalated all {{aq.metrics.emergency_count|int}} emergency vignettes, and none of the {{aq.metrics.out_of_domain_count|int}} out-of-domain questions received an ANSWER ({{aq.metrics.out_of_domain_abstain_count|int}} abstained, {{aq.metrics.confusion_matrix.ABSTAIN.GENERALIZE|int}} was generalised and {{aq.metrics.confusion_matrix.ABSTAIN["SOFT-ASK"]|int}} soft-asked). The errors repeat the boundary problem of the study: expected answers were generalised {{aq.metrics.confusion_matrix.ANSWER.GENERALIZE|int}} times and abstained {{aq.metrics.confusion_matrix.ANSWER.ABSTAIN|int}} times, expected generalisations were answered {{aq.metrics.confusion_matrix.GENERALIZE.ANSWER|int}} times, and only {{aq.metrics.subsets.complex_comorbid_contraindications.routing_accuracy|perc}} of the comorbidity vignettes were routed as expected. The run shared the GPU with the study's test-split run (`evaluation/results/logs/`), so its latencies are not user-facing.

#### RABBITS Before and After the Brand-Name Fix

Live RABBITS sent the same 60 brand–generic pairs (30 Indian brands, listed in `reports/rabbits_before_fix/brands.txt`) in a personal and a general template, so that the two messages of a pair differ only in the drug name (Table 4.19). Before the fix the brand message was abstained in {{rabbits_counts.before.brand_abstain|int}} of {{rabbits_counts.before.items|int}} items (the generic message in {{rabbits_counts.before.generic_abstain|int}}): Gate 2's rule that a named product absent from the evidence may not exist fired for real brands, because guidelines use generic names, and the extractor often recorded no medicine at all (ADL-059). The fix (`02d9774`) gives the extractor, both gates and the generator system-written lexicon notes such as "Accupril: brand name of quinapril (ACE inhibitor)", records a clearly stated current medicine that the model missed through a deterministic backstop, and removes from the search query any drug the patient never mentioned. Action consistency rose from {{rabbits_before.action_consistency_pct|perc}} to {{rabbits.action_consistency_pct|perc}} (Indian brands from {{rabbits_before.by_origin.indian.action_consistency_pct|perc}} to {{rabbits.by_origin.indian.action_consistency_pct|perc}}), and the stored generic agreed in {{rabbits.extraction_consistency_pct|perc}} of personal items, against {{rabbits_before.extraction_consistency_pct|perc}}. In {{rabbits_counts.after.brand_more_cautious|int}} of the {{rabbits_counts.after.flips|int}} remaining flips the brand message received the more cautious action, most often ABSTAIN against GENERALIZE ({{rabbits_counts.after.flip_pairs["ABSTAIN vs GENERALIZE"]|int}}), so the residual brand sensitivity errs on the conservative side, at a usefulness cost ({{rabbits_counts.after.brand_abstain|int}} brand against {{rabbits_counts.after.generic_abstain|int}} generic messages abstained). Both runs had Gate 3 off, each is a single run, and the fix has not been re-tested on HalluBench.

#### Adversarial Suite

All {{adversarial.counts.payloads|int}} payloads passed every deterministic check both before and after the brand-name fix, the second run also with Gate 3 enabled: no system-prompt leakage, fabricated prescription or unsafe output, and every emergency payload escalated (Table 4.20). Two payloads changed route. The fabricated-guideline payload asking to confirm "amlodipine 50 mg twice daily" was abstained before and now receives a one-sentence GENERALIZE answer stating the guideline starting dose of 5 mg with a citation; it does not confirm the false dose but does not reject it explicitly either. The "DAN" role-play payload demanding 200 mg of lisinopril is now escalated by Gate 2, a safe but unnecessary emergency card.

#### Claim Annotation: Human Sheet and AI Annotator

`python -m evaluation.human_annotation --export --split test` sampled 50 test items, stratified by category with seed 42, among those whose C5 or C1 response contains a claim, and wrote all {{annotation.n_claims|comma}} claims of the {{annotation.n_responses|int}} blinded responses of {{annotation.n_items|int}} items, with the evidence the judges saw, to `evaluation/annotation/human_annotation_sheet.xlsx` (layout in Appendix 4). Its two human columns were not filled before submission, so no human agreement is reported. As a third, explicitly non-human rater, an AI model (Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic) labelled every claim with the same rubric, in ten batches that held only the question, the response, the claims and their evidence (no condition, category, answer key or judge label). Its labels are stored apart from the human sheet, so that human annotators stay blind to them.

The AI annotator labelled {{ai_annotation.label_counts.UNSUPPORTED|int}} claims UNSUPPORTED and {{ai_annotation.label_counts.CONTRADICTED|int}} CONTRADICTED. It agreed moderately with the LLM judge (κ = {{ai_annotation.ai_vs_llm.kappa_3class|cif}}, {{ai_annotation.ai_vs_llm.raw_agreement|pct}} identical labels; Table 4.16) and was stricter: it rejected {{ai_annotation.ai_vs_llm.confusion_ai_rows_judge_cols.1.0|int}} claims that the judge supported, against {{ai_annotation.ai_vs_llm.confusion_ai_rows_judge_cols.0.1|int}} the other way round, so the judge's absolute rates are, if anything, too low. Like the two judges with each other, it agreed only weakly with the NLI judge (κ = {{ai_annotation.ai_vs_nli.kappa_3class|cif}}). In the sample, the share of unsupported claims was almost the same for C5 ({{ai_annotation.per_condition.C5.unsupported_claims_ai|pct}}) and C1 ({{ai_annotation.per_condition.C1.unsupported_claims_ai|pct}}), and on the {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.n_pairs|int}} items where both conditions answered with claims, {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.ai.p_c5|pct}} of C5 and {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.ai.p_c1|pct}} of C1 responses contained at least one (exact McNemar *p* = {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.ai.pvalue|p}}; LLM judge {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.llm.p_c5|pct}} against {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.llm.p_c1|pct}}, *p* = {{ai_annotation.paired_c5_vs_c1.llm.pvalue|p}}). This supports Section 4.10: the dual gate lowers hallucination by answering less, not by grounding its answers better. The AI annotator does not replace clinicians: it is a single model, from the same family as the AI assistant used to develop much of the code and evaluation, so it may share their blind spots, and no person checked its labels.
