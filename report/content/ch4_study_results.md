## Hallucination Study

This section answers RQ1 to RQ3 with the design of Section 3.6. The test split of MedBridge-HalluBench ({{study.per_condition.C5.n_items|int}} items, {{study.per_condition.C5.n_turns|int}} item-turns) was run under ten conditions: C0 to C5, the reference C1G (production prompt, no gates) and the variants V1, V2 and V12, all with `qwen3.5:4b` (Section 4.5). Three degraded turns, one each in C0, V1 and V12, are excluded (Appendix 1), far below the 5 per cent refusal threshold. The `gemma3:12b` LLM judge scored every condition (with two C0 failures). By the project owner's decision, the NLI judge was stopped early. It scored C0, C1 and C1G completely and {{study.per_condition.C2.judge_coverage.nli.judged|int}} C2 responses, and it is used only to measure inter-judge agreement. All primary metrics, tests and intervals therefore come from the LLM judge. Study latencies come from a shared GPU and are not user-facing (Appendix 1).

#### Routing and Emergency Handling

C5 routed {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.accuracy_strict|ci}} of turns exactly (lenient {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.accuracy_lenient|pct}}, macro-F1 {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.macro_f1|f2}}). Each gate alone did worse (macro-F1 {{study.per_condition.C3.routing_system.macro_f1|f2}} for C3 and {{study.per_condition.C4.routing_system.macro_f1|f2}} for C4). Every condition with the fast path escalated all {{study.per_condition.C5.emergency.recall_system.n|int}} emergency turns and never escalated a non-emergency turn (0 of {{study.per_condition.C5.emergency.false_escalation_system.n|int}} in C5). The main weakness is the boundary between ANSWER and GENERALIZE. Of {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.per_class.ANSWER.support|int}} gold-ANSWER turns, C5 answered {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.confusion.0.0|int}} and generalised {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.confusion.0.2|int}}. It also answered {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.confusion.2.0|int}} of {{study.per_condition.C5.routing_system.per_class.GENERALIZE.support|int}} gold-GENERALIZE turns.

#### Hallucination and Unsafe Answers

The LLM judge found at least one unsupported or contradicted claim in {{study.per_condition.C0.llm.hallucination|pct}} of C0 responses, {{study.per_condition.C1.llm.hallucination|pct}} for C1, {{study.per_condition.C2.llm.hallucination|pct}} for C2 and {{study.per_condition.C5.llm.hallucination|ci}} for C5. C1G, with the production prompt but no gates, was no better than C1 ({{study.per_condition.C1G.llm.hallucination|pct}}). The NLI judge, which scored only C0, C1 and C1G completely, also rated C0 worst ({{study.per_condition.C0.nli.hallucination|pct}}) and gave C1 and C1G higher rates than the LLM judge did ({{study.per_condition.C1.nli.hallucination|pct}} each).

The reduction comes mainly from abstaining, not from better-grounded answers. Templates contain no claims and count as not hallucinated. C5 made claims in {{study.per_condition.C5.llm.hallucination_given_claims.n|int}} of {{study.per_condition.C5.llm.n_judged|int}} turns, against {{study.per_condition.C1.llm.hallucination_given_claims.n|int}} for C1. Among those responses, hallucination was unchanged: {{study.per_condition.C1.llm.hallucination_given_claims|pct}} for C1 and {{study.per_condition.C5.llm.hallucination_given_claims|pct}} for C5. The claim ratios of C1 ({{study.per_condition.C1.llm.unsupported_claim_ratio|cif}}) and C5 ({{study.per_condition.C5.llm.unsupported_claim_ratio|cif}}) overlap. The gates therefore act as *selective abstention*. Only Gate 3 lowered hallucination among responses with claims, to {{study.per_condition.V2.llm.hallucination_given_claims|pct}} for V2.

#### Coverage and the Contribution of Each Gate

The price of these gains is coverage. The share of answerable turns answered fell from {{study.per_condition.C1G.coverage_answerable|pct}} (C1G) to {{study.per_condition.C5.coverage_answerable|pct}} (C5) and {{study.per_condition.V2.coverage_answerable|pct}} (V2). In C5 the Context Gate turns some refusals into clarification questions ({{study.per_condition.C5.behaviour_distribution.ASKED_CLARIFICATION|int}} against {{study.per_condition.C4.behaviour_distribution.ASKED_CLARIFICATION|int}} in C4). This also lowers appropriate abstention on unanswerable turns ({{study.per_condition.C5.appropriate_abstention|pct}} against {{study.per_condition.C4.appropriate_abstention|pct}}). In the risk-coverage plot [@geifman2017selective], every RAG condition from C1 to C5 has about 80 to 85 per cent risk among answered turns (C5 {{study.per_condition.C5.risk_coverage.risk|pct}}). Only V2 ({{study.per_condition.V2.risk_coverage.risk|pct}}) and V12 lie lower. Ranking answers by maximum reranker score hardly lowers risk, which fits the null result of V1 (its only active signal is a rerank threshold).

The waterfall is anchored at C1G, which shares the production prompt with C3 to C5. In its descriptive, unpaired steps, the Context Gate lowered hallucination by 16.7 percentage points and the Evidence Gate removed a further 20.1. The Evidence Gate alone (C4) removed 30.5. In a paired test against C1G, the full gate changed hallucination by {{study.tests.mcnemar.16.risk_difference.estimate|pp}} (Holm *p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.16.pvalue_holm|p}}) and unsafe answers by {{study.tests.mcnemar.17.risk_difference.estimate|pp}} (*p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.17.pvalue_holm|p}}). The Context Gate's gain on top of the Evidence Gate is small and fragile. C5 against C4 is significant per turn ({{study.tests.mcnemar.4.risk_difference.estimate|pp}}, *p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.4.pvalue_holm|p}}) but not per item (*p* = {{study.tests.sensitivity_item_level.4.pvalue_holm|p}}), and it shows no significant effect on unsafe answers (*p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.9.pvalue_holm|p}}).

#### Calibration, Decision Rule and Variants

All thresholds were fitted on the development split, and every V1, V2 and V12 test turn recorded them. For V1, C5 already made no false ANSWER on that split, so the objective was tied at zero and the tie-breaks kept only the ANSWER rerank threshold (2.072). On the development split V1 matched C5's false-ANSWER rate (0) and coverage (70.4 per cent) and raised exact routing accuracy from 69.5 to 71.2 per cent. For V2, θ maximised sentence-level κ against the LLM judge (κ = {{calibration.v2.kappa_sentence|f3}}). τ = 1 is the calibration's fallback, because no τ kept 85 per cent of C5's development coverage ({{calibration.v2.dev_result.coverage|pct}} against a floor of {{calibration.v2.coverage_floor|pct}}). In the studied code it strips unsupported sentences without ever withdrawing an answer (Section 4.12).

[[table:calibration|Thresholds calibrated on the development split]]

The decision rule of Section 3.6 required Holm-adjusted *p* < 0.05 for C5 against both C1 and C2, on both outcomes. All four tests meet it, so the fallback is not triggered, and RQ1 is answered positively for the LLM judge. A confirmation by the second (NLI) judge was not possible, because the early-concluded NLI run produced no test-split judgments for C5 (its 59 C5 judgments are development-split calibration data). The decision holds with degraded turns included, without the {{study.sensitivity.without_prompt_overlap.n_items_removed|int}} `prompt_overlap` items (C5 against C1: *p* = {{study.sensitivity.without_prompt_overlap.tests.1.pvalue_holm|p}}), with the non-clinical claim filter disabled (hallucination only, because that view does not recompute unsafe answers: C5 against C1 *p* = {{study.sensitivity.filter_disabled.tests.1.pvalue_holm|p}}, against C2 *p* = {{study.sensitivity.filter_disabled.tests.2.pvalue_holm|p}}) and at item level (*p* = {{study.tests.sensitivity_item_level.1.pvalue_holm|p}}) (Appendix 1).

As Section 3.6 allows, the variants were evaluated anyway. V1 had no measurable effect ({{study.tests.mcnemar.10.risk_difference.estimate|pp}}, *p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.10.pvalue_holm|p}}). It was also slower: its imputed median was {{study.per_condition.V1.latency.imputed.p50_ms|s}} s against {{study.per_condition.C5.latency.imputed.p50_ms|s}} s in the study runs. V2 lowered hallucination to {{study.per_condition.V2.llm.hallucination|ci}} ({{study.tests.mcnemar.11.risk_difference.estimate|pp}}, b = {{study.tests.mcnemar.11.b_treatment_only|int}}, c = {{study.tests.mcnemar.11.c_control_only|int}}, *p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.11.pvalue_holm|p}}). This held with degraded turns included (*p* = {{study.sensitivity.including_degraded.tests.11.pvalue_holm|p}}), without the `prompt_overlap` items (*p* = {{study.sensitivity.without_prompt_overlap.tests.11.pvalue_holm|p}}) and with the claim filter disabled (*p* = {{study.sensitivity.filter_disabled.tests.6.pvalue_holm|p}}). The item-level analysis covers only C5 against C0 to C4. Its effect on unsafe answers was not significant ({{study.per_condition.V2.llm.unsafe_answer|pct}} against {{study.per_condition.C5.llm.unsafe_answer|pct}}, *p* = {{study.tests.mcnemar.14.pvalue_holm|p}}). V12 behaved like V2 but was not tested against it.

Applying by analogy the brief's rule "default on if it wins" (stated for the fallback, which was not triggered), Gate 3 is now enabled by default with θ = 0.2 and τ = 1.0 (commit `7a3bc67`), and V1 stays off. Four caveats apply. V2's coverage fell to {{study.per_condition.V2.coverage_answerable|pct}}, a drop that was not tested. Part of its effect comes from shorter answers ({{study.per_condition.V2.llm.claims_per_response|f2}} claims per response against {{study.per_condition.C5.llm.claims_per_response|f2}}, with an overlapping claim ratio). θ was fitted on 59 turns against the same judge, and no second judge confirms the effect. Finally, the deployed code, which also contains the later changes of Section 4.12, is not the studied code.

#### Error Analysis and Threats to Validity

The error analysis (`evaluation/results/error_analysis.md`) shows four patterns (synthetic items):

1. **Gate 1 reframes personal questions.** In 12 C5 turns with gold SOFT-ASK, all missing-context items, Gate 1 proceeded. For example, it treated "what blood pressure should i be aiming for?" (HB-0068) as a general question. Three of these are follow-ups in which the user declines or cannot give the details (HB-0099 and HB-0103 refuse; in HB-0100 the user does not know the risk score), where the gold label is debatable.
2. **Gate 2 checks topic, not personalisation.** It answered three gold SOFT-ASK missing-context turns because "the evidence directly addresses" the topic (for example HB-0084, on home BP machines), and answered a fourth (HB-0104, third turn) specifically although the user had asked for general information.
3. **Residual unsafe answers pass through GENERALIZE.** Nine of the ten were generalisations. Six of the ten answered false premises; the five of these that were generalised still made unsupported claims. For an invented anticoagulant (HB-0140), Gate 2's rationale took the name for dabigatran. V2 cut the reply to two sentences, but the shorter reply still treated the drug as real. Gate 3 cannot remove a false premise that the remaining text accepts.
4. **The gates pay off where nothing should be said.** Out-of-domain unsafe answers fell from {{study.by_category.out_of_domain.per_condition.C1.unsafe_llm|pct}} (C1) to {{study.by_category.out_of_domain.per_condition.C5.unsafe_llm|pct}} in C5 (Holm *p* = {{study.by_category.out_of_domain.tests.10.pvalue_holm|p}}). For a child's syrup dose (HB-0195), Gate 2 abstained by template, whereas C1's refusal still carried referral advice judged unsupported. On general-topic questions, which the gates rightly let through, they changed nothing (*p* = {{study.by_category.general_topic.tests.8.pvalue_holm|p}} against C1G). Only the grounding verifier lowered hallucination there: V2 to {{study.by_category.general_topic.per_condition.V2.hallucination_llm|pct}} (Holm *p* = {{study.by_category.general_topic.tests.6.pvalue_holm|p}}) and V12 to {{study.by_category.general_topic.per_condition.V12.hallucination_llm|pct}} (Holm *p* = {{study.by_category.general_topic.tests.7.pvalue_holm|p}}).

The rubric inflates absolute rates: restated user facts ("The user's blood pressure reading is 146/94 mmHg", HB-0003) and generic hedges are labelled UNSUPPORTED, and labels vary between batches (an almost identical sentence in HB-0068 was supported in C5 but not in V2). Paired differences are therefore more trustworthy than absolute levels, and no rate here is a clinical error rate.

Beyond the limitations in Section 4.12 (synthetic benchmark, one judge for the gated conditions, no human labels), four threats are specific to this study. The response-level rate mixes abstention with grounding. V2's θ was fitted against the judge that scored the test split. Each condition was run once, with only 66 unanswerable turns, and C5's development and test rates differ (25.4 against 37.2 per cent). Finally, the study ran on an Apple M5, not on the RTX 4050 target, from commits with uncommitted changes (`82359eb`, `085eb53`, `3f58b59`).
